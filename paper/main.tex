\begin{abstract}
Reasoning systems must decide when to spend effort checking their own work. We study this decision in a small testbed where everything is exactly measurable. An agent executes an arithmetic chain that may contain one silent fault, sees a noisy one-bit cue per step, and divides a fixed budget between executing, verifying and repairing steps. We train Double DQN agents with outcome-only and process rewards and several observation encoders, and evaluate them on chains up to $2.3\times$ longer than in training, with 10 seeds and paired tests.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Rewarding informative checks helps agents acquire and retain checking. With length-invariant observations, \val{main/outcome_inv/collapse_rate/pct} of outcome-only agents' late checkpoints collapse to low success, mostly by abandoning checking, against \val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/pct} under a reward that pays for checks. Any term that pays for checks recovers most of this benefit on training lengths, but only the cue-specific terms keep checking frugal enough to work on long chains.
  \item Length generalization depends mainly on the observation encoder. With the original encoder's length-dependent features, every held-out observation is novel and every reward falls below a never-check baseline on lengths 10--14. With length-invariant features, the same reward keeps \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_long/success} success at lengths 7--9 and \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14. Removing only the position features is not enough.
  \item Under every reward, agents repeatedly fall into policies that detect faults but do not repair them. This is a learning failure, not reward hacking. Paying the fault bonus at the repair step removes it (\val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/pct} $\to$ \val{followups/ma_repair/collapse_rate/pct} of late checkpoints) and makes the last checkpoint reliable.
\end{enumerate}
These results revise an earlier version of this study, which attributed the length-generalization failure to the reward.
\end{abstract}
