\begin{abstract}
Reasoning systems must decide when to spend effort checking their own work. We study this decision in a small testbed where everything is exactly measurable: an agent executes an arithmetic chain that may contain one silent fault, sees a noisy one-bit cue per step, and divides a fixed budget between executing, verifying and repairing steps. We train Double DQN agents with outcome-only and process rewards and several observation encoders, and evaluate them on chains up to $2.3\times$ longer than in training (10 seeds, paired tests).
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Length generalization depends mainly on the observation encoder. With the original encoder's length-dependent features, every reward falls below a never-check baseline on the longest chains. With length-invariant features, the reward that pays most for informative checks keeps \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_long/success} success at lengths 7--9 and \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14 (checkpoints selected on training lengths), against \val{main/optimal/heldout_long/success} and \val{main/optimal/heldout_xlong/success} for an exact planner; on the longest chains a cue-triggered heuristic (\val{main/cue_triggered/heldout_xlong/success}) still beats it.
  \item Under every reward, agents drift into policies that catch faults but do not repair them. This is a learning failure under partial observability, not reward hacking: one observation bit recording that a caught fault is still unrepaired removes it under all three rewards. A window of the agent's own last 3 actions does the same; a window of 8, which also reveals position, fails on long chains, where its windows are unfamiliar.
  \item Paying for informative checks makes checking policies appear sooner and collapse less often: \val{main/outcome_inv/collapse_rate/pct} of outcome-only agents' checkpoints after episode 5{,}000 fall back to success $\le 0.35$, against \val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/pct} with the check payment, also under three other learner settings. A step-correctness bonus, closer to a process reward model, helps more the larger it is, also when it cannot be farmed by redoing steps, but its checkpoints plateau lower.
\end{enumerate}
These results revise an earlier version of this study, which attributed the length-generalization failure to the reward.
\end{abstract}
