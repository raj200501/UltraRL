\title{Learning When to Verify Under a Budget:\\Process Rewards, Representations, and Length Generalization}

\author{Raj Kashikar}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Reasoning systems must decide not only how to solve a problem but also when to spend effort checking their own work. We study this decision in a small testbed where everything of interest is exactly measurable. An agent executes an arithmetic chain that may contain one silent fault, sees a noisy one-bit cue per step, and divides a fixed budget between executing, verifying and repairing steps. We train small Double DQN agents with outcome-only, naive process and monitorability-aware rewards and several observation encoders, and evaluate them on chains up to $2.3\times$ longer than in training, with 10 seeds and paired statistics. We report four findings.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Process rewards make checking learnable and keep it from collapsing. With length-invariant observations, outcome-only agents find checking policies but have abandoned them in \val{main/outcome_inv/collapse_nevercheck_rate/pct} of late checkpoints, against \val{main/ma_inv/collapse_nevercheck_rate/pct} under the monitorability-aware reward. Ablations attribute nearly all of that reward's benefit to one term that pays for checking cued steps.
  \item The reward is partly gamed. It pays for \emph{catching} a fault, and agents repeatedly drift into policies that detect faults without repairing them. Paying for the repair instead removes this collapse (\val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/pct} $\to$ \val{followups/ma_repair/collapse_rate/pct} of late checkpoints) and makes the last checkpoint far more reliable.
  \item Length generalization is decided mainly by representation. With absolute position features every held-out observation is novel, and every reward scheme falls \emph{below} a never-check baseline on lengths 10--14. With length-invariant features the same reward keeps \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_long/success} success at lengths 7--9, within a few points of a hand-coded checker, and \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14. A feature that adds information but goes out of range on long chains made generalization worse, as an observation-coverage analysis predicted before the experiment.
  \item DQN policies churn throughout training, so last-checkpoint evaluation understates what runs learn.
\end{enumerate}
These results revise an earlier version of this study, which attributed the length-generalization failure to the reward.
\end{abstract}
