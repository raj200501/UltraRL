\begin{abstract}
Reasoning systems must decide when to spend effort checking their own work. We study this decision in a small testbed where everything is exactly measurable. An agent executes an arithmetic chain that may contain one silent fault, sees a noisy one-bit cue per step, and divides a fixed budget between executing, verifying and repairing steps. We train Double DQN agents with outcome-only and process rewards and several observation encoders, and evaluate them on chains up to $2.3\times$ longer than in training, with 10 seeds and paired tests.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Paying for informative checks helps agents keep checking. With length-invariant observations, \val{main/outcome_inv/collapse_rate/pct} of outcome-only agents' late checkpoints collapse to low success, mostly by abandoning checking. The figure is \val{main/naive_inv/collapse_rate/pct} with a token check payment and \val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/pct} with a substantial one, and the gap between outcome-only and substantial payment holds under three other learner settings. On long chains, how checks are paid matters: a term that rewards closing unchecked stretches drives indiscriminate checking that exhausts the budget, unless cue-specific terms offset it.
  \item Length generalization depends mainly on the observation encoder. With the original encoder's length-dependent features, every held-out observation is novel and every reward falls below a never-check baseline on lengths 10--14. With length-invariant features, the same reward keeps \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_long/success} success at lengths 7--9 and \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14, against \val{main/optimal/heldout_long/success} and \val{main/optimal/heldout_xlong/success} for an exact planner that knows the chain length. Removing only the position features is not enough.
  \item Under every reward, agents repeatedly fall into policies that detect faults but do not repair them. This is a learning failure, not reward hacking: repairing earns more under every reward, but after a caught fault the learned values of repairing and of continuing differ by a median of \val{probe/ma_inv/q_gap}, while their true returns differ by \val{probe/ma_inv/true_gap}. Paying the fault bonus at the repair step widens that gap and removes the failure (\val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/pct} $\to$ \val{followups/ma_repair/collapse_rate/pct} of late checkpoints), so the last checkpoint becomes reliable; the best checkpoints are no better, and possibly slightly worse, on held-out lengths.
\end{enumerate}
These results revise an earlier version of this study, which attributed the length-generalization failure to the reward.
\end{abstract}
