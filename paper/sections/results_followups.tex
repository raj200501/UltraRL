\subsection{Follow-up interventions}
\label{sec:followups}

We test the explanations suggested by the analyses above. Each intervention is added to the monitorability-aware agent with invariant observations, 10 seeds each, and compared with the matching reference agent on paired episodes (Tables~\ref{tab:followups} and~\ref{tab:pending}); the repair-pending bit and the action histories are also trained under the other two rewards. These comparisons are exploratory and their $p$-values are not corrected for multiplicity. In brief: a bit that records whether a caught fault is still unrepaired removes the detect-without-repair collapse under every reward. A history of the agent's own last 3 actions all but removes collapse under every reward and, under the monitorability-aware reward, generalizes to long chains at the last checkpoint, whereas a history of the last 8 actions, which on training lengths also reveals position, fails on long chains, where many of its windows are unfamiliar. Paying the fault bonus at the repair step is a reward-side remedy for the same failure. A memory of having seen a fault removes wasted \texttt{REVISE}s but not the failure, and a slack-per-remaining-step feature made the repair decision worse (Appendix~\ref{app:followups}).

\paragraph{A memory bit removes most wasted \texttt{REVISE}s.}
Adding one bit that records whether an \textsc{unsafe} check has been seen removes the aliasing between repairs and no-ops. Wasted \texttt{REVISE}s at 10--14 fall from \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/nonrepair_revise_count} to \val{followups/ma_memory/heldout_xlong/nonrepair_revise_count} per episode. Training-length success rises by $+$\val{diff/ma_memory-ma_inv/train_like/success} (\val{diff/ma_memory-ma_inv/train_like/success/npos}/10 seeds, $p=\val{diff/ma_memory-ma_inv/train_like/success/p}$). Held-out success at selected checkpoints does not change resolvably ($p=\val{diff/ma_memory-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success/p}$ at 10--14), because the agent spends the recovered slack on more un-cued checks (\val{followups/ma_memory/heldout_xlong/verify_rate_cue0} vs.\ \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/verify_rate_cue0}). The allocation explanation of \S\ref{sec:residual} is therefore only partly confirmed: removing one source of waste was not enough. Checkpoint collapse is lower with the memory bit (\val{followups/ma_memory/collapse_rate/pct} vs.\ \val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/pct}), but the intervals overlap. At the last checkpoint the point estimates favour the bit by $+$\val{diff/ma_memory@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success} at 7--9 and $+$\val{diff/ma_memory@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14, similar in size to the repair payment's gain below, but 10 seeds resolve neither ($p=\val{diff/ma_memory@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success/p}$ and $\val{diff/ma_memory@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_xlong/success/p}$). The bit is not a test of the aliasing explanation of \S\ref{sec:norepair}: it stays set after a repair, so after continuing past a caught fault the agent still sees observations that also occur on track (\val{probe/ma_memory/aliased_rate/pct} of them, against \val{probe/ma_inv/aliased_rate/pct} without the bit). The repair-pending bit below is that test.

\paragraph{A repair-pending bit removes the failure under every reward.}
This bit is set by an \textsc{unsafe} check and cleared once the located step has been correctly re-executed, so it records exactly what the invariant observation hides; an agent that remembered its own checks and undos could compute it. We train it with all three rewards (Table~\ref{tab:pending}). Late checkpoints that detect faults without repairing them fall from \val{main/outcome_inv/collapse_norepair_rate/pct}, \val{main/naive_inv/collapse_norepair_rate/pct} and \val{main/ma_inv/collapse_norepair_rate/pct} (outcome-only, naive and monitorability-aware) to \val{followups/outcome_pending/collapse_norepair_rate/pct}, \val{followups/naive_pending/collapse_norepair_rate/pct} and \val{followups/ma_pending/collapse_norepair_rate/pct}. The greedy choice after an \textsc{unsafe} result becomes stable: the last checkpoints continue past a caught fault at \val{probe/outcome_pending@final/think_rate/pct}, \val{probe/naive_pending@final/think_rate/pct} and \val{probe/ma_pending@final/think_rate/pct} of such points. The bit does not make the values accurate (continuing is still overvalued by \val{probe/ma_pending/think_bias} for the monitorability-aware agents). Removing the aliasing is therefore enough to stabilise the choice, but aliasing is not sufficient for instability: the repair payment below also stabilises it, although with that reward \val{probe/ma_repair/aliased_rate/pct} of the continuing-branch observations still occur on track.

The bit also changes how often outcome-only agents collapse at all. Outcome-only agents now collapse in \val{followups/outcome_pending/collapse_rate/pct} [\val{followups/outcome_pending/collapse_rate/lo/pct}, \val{followups/outcome_pending/collapse_rate/hi/pct}] of late checkpoints instead of \val{main/outcome_inv/collapse_rate/pct}, and fall into the low-detection regime in \val{followups/outcome_pending/collapse_lowdetect_rate/pct} instead of \val{main/outcome_inv/collapse_lowdetect_rate/pct}, presumably because checks pay off only once the repair that follows them is learned reliably. What this means for check payments depends on the metric. Their advantage in collapse shrinks with the bit (\val{followups/outcome_pending/collapse_rate/pct} against \val{followups/ma_pending/collapse_rate/pct} of late checkpoints, from \val{main/outcome_inv/collapse_rate/pct} against \val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/pct} without it), so part of what they buy in \S\ref{sec:learnability} is compensation for the missing information. Their gain in late training-length success does not shrink: with the bit, outcome-only agents behave much like monitorability-aware agents without it (collapse \val{followups/outcome_pending/collapse_rate/pct} against \val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/pct}; mean late training-length success \val{followups/outcome_pending/late_success} against \val{main/ma_inv/late_success}), and the check payment still adds about as much on top (\val{followups/ma_pending/late_success}). With the bit, the check payment shows up in training stability rather than in peak performance. At selected checkpoints the two rewards are indistinguishable on held-out lengths (monitorability-aware minus outcome-only: \val{diff/ma_pending-outcome_pending/heldout_long/success} [\val{diff/ma_pending-outcome_pending/heldout_long/success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_pending-outcome_pending/heldout_long/success/hi}] at 7--9 and \val{diff/ma_pending-outcome_pending/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14). But outcome-only agents still collapse more often (\val{followups/outcome_pending/collapse_rate/pct} of late checkpoints against \val{followups/ma_pending/collapse_rate/pct}), and their last checkpoints are much worse (\val{followups/outcome_pending@final/heldout_long/success} against \val{followups/ma_pending@final/heldout_long/success} at 7--9, $p=\val{diff/ma_pending@final-outcome_pending@final/heldout_long/success/p}$), already on training lengths (\val{followups/outcome_pending@final/train_like/success} against \val{followups/ma_pending@final/train_like/success}), so only part of that gap is a generalization deficit. With the bit, the monitorability-aware agents' last checkpoints are slightly worse than their selected ones on training lengths (\val{diff/ma_pending@final-ma_pending/train_like/success}, \val{diff/ma_pending@final-ma_pending/train_like/success/nneg}/10 seeds lower), better on held-out lengths ($+$\val{diff/ma_pending@final-ma_pending/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14, $p=\val{diff/ma_pending@final-ma_pending/heldout_xlong/success/p}$), and better than the reference's last checkpoints (by \val{diff/ma_pending@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success} at 7--9, $p=\val{diff/ma_pending@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success/p}$). At selected checkpoints, by contrast, the bit lowers the monitorability-aware agents' success on the longest chains, most likely because selection on saturated training-length validation picks checkpoints that over-check (Appendix~\ref{app:followups}).

\paragraph{A short history of recent actions all but removes collapse; a long one fails on long chains.}
The pending bit is computed by the environment. To test whether a learner can do without it, we instead add the agent's recent actions and check results to the invariant observation, one-hot coded and zero-padded at the start of an episode: either the last 8 actions or only the last 3 (Table~\ref{tab:pending}; the 3-action window was added after seeing the 8-action results). The two windows carry different information. Three actions show a caught fault until it is repaired or for two steps after it is skipped, and whether a \texttt{REVISE} followed a \textsc{safe} or an \textsc{unsafe} check, but little about position. Eight actions hold the whole episode so far at \val{agentcov/ma_history/train_like/whole_window_rate/pct} of training-length decision points, so they also reveal position (the number of steps executed) and the full record of checks, which the invariant encoder was built to hide. Neither window shows the chain length, so neither says much more than the invariant observation about how many steps remain. The networks also differ in size (\val{main/ma_inv/parameters} parameters for the invariant observation, \val{followups/ma_history3/parameters} with 3 actions and \val{followups/ma_history/parameters} with 8).

On training lengths both windows all but remove collapse under every reward (\val{followups/outcome_history3/collapse_rate/pct}, \val{followups/naive_history3/collapse_rate/pct} and \val{followups/ma_history3/collapse_rate/pct} of late checkpoints with 3 actions, for outcome-only, naive and monitorability-aware rewards; \val{followups/outcome_history/collapse_rate/pct}, \val{followups/naive_history/collapse_rate/pct} and \val{followups/ma_history/collapse_rate/pct} with 8), more completely than the pending bit does for outcome-only agents (\val{followups/outcome_pending/collapse_rate/pct}). Selected checkpoints reach \val{followups/histories/train_like/min}--\val{followups/histories/train_like/max} training-length success. The check payment's advantage in late training-length success shrinks as the observation carries more: it is $+$\val{diff/ma_inv-outcome_inv/train_like/late_success} without any addition, $+$\val{diff/ma_pending-outcome_pending/train_like/late_success} with the pending bit, $+$\val{diff/ma_history3-outcome_history3/train_like/late_success} [\val{diff/ma_history3-outcome_history3/train_like/late_success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_history3-outcome_history3/train_like/late_success/hi}] with 3 actions and $+$\val{diff/ma_history-outcome_history/train_like/late_success} with 8, positive in \val{diff/ma_history-outcome_history/train_like/late_success/npos}/10 seeds even then ($p=\val{diff/ma_history-outcome_history/train_like/late_success/p}$); with 8 actions the check-paid agents also still first exceed 0.5 earlier (median episode \val{followups/ma_history/first_above_half_median} against \val{followups/outcome_history/first_above_half_median}, $p=\val{diff/ma_history-outcome_history/train_like/first_above_half/p}$). Most of the shrinkage thus already happens with the short window, so position and the full record of checks can account for at most the remaining difference between the two windows. Absolute gaps are also compressed near the ceiling. As a ratio of late failure rates, outcome-only agents fail \val{ratio/inv/late_failure} times as often as check-paid ones without any addition and \val{ratio/history/late_failure} times as often with 8 actions (\val{ratio/pending/late_failure} with the pending bit, \val{ratio/history3/late_failure} with 3 actions), so on that scale no observation removes the payment's advantage. The value probe shows that the longer window changes what the agents learn: after a caught fault, the 3-action agents overvalue continuing about as much as the pending-bit agents (excess errors \val{probe/outcome_history3/think_bias}, \val{probe/naive_history3/think_bias} and \val{probe/ma_history3/think_bias}), the 8-action agents much less (\val{probe/outcome_history/think_bias}, \val{probe/naive_history/think_bias} and \val{probe/ma_history/think_bias}).

On long chains the short window holds up better at the last checkpoint, but only the monitorability-aware agents actually transfer better. At 10--14 the 3-action agents' last checkpoints beat the same rewards without a history by $+$\val{diff/outcome_history3@final-outcome_inv@final/heldout_xlong/success}, $+$\val{diff/naive_history3@final-naive_inv@final/heldout_xlong/success} and $+$\val{diff/ma_history3@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_xlong/success}. For outcome-only and naive rewards this mostly reflects training stability: the lead is larger on training lengths ($+$\val{diff/outcome_history3@final-outcome_inv@final/train_like/success} and $+$\val{diff/naive_history3@final-naive_inv@final/train_like/success}), where the agents without a history have mostly collapsed, and the 3-action agents fall from \val{followups/outcome_history3@final/train_like/success} and \val{followups/naive_history3@final/train_like/success} to \val{followups/outcome_history3@final/heldout_xlong/success} and \val{followups/naive_history3@final/heldout_xlong/success}. The monitorability-aware ones fall only from \val{followups/ma_history3@final/train_like/success} to \ci{followups/ma_history3@final/heldout_xlong/success}, about as well as with the pending bit (\val{followups/ma_pending@final/heldout_xlong/success}) and within an unresolved \val{diff/ma_history3@final-cue_triggered/heldout_xlong/success} [\val{diff/ma_history3@final-cue_triggered/heldout_xlong/success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_history3@final-cue_triggered/heldout_xlong/success/hi}] of the cue-triggered heuristic (a comparison made after seeing the results). At selected checkpoints no 3-action agent does better than the agents without a history at 10--14 (\val{diff/outcome_history3-outcome_inv/heldout_xlong/success}, \val{diff/naive_history3-naive_inv/heldout_xlong/success} and \val{diff/ma_history3-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success}, none resolved); for the monitorability-aware agents this is most likely the selection effect described for the pending bit (selected \val{followups/ma_history3/heldout_xlong/success}, last \val{followups/ma_history3@final/heldout_xlong/success}). With 8 actions, even fault-free chains succeed only \val{followups/outcome_history/heldout_xlong/clean_success}, \val{followups/naive_history/heldout_xlong/clean_success} and \val{followups/ma_history/heldout_xlong/clean_success} of the time at 10--14, and \val{followups/outcome_history/heldout_xlong/incomplete_commit/pct}, \val{followups/naive_history/heldout_xlong/incomplete_commit/pct} and \val{followups/ma_history/heldout_xlong/incomplete_commit/pct} of episodes end before the chain is complete (with 3 actions, \val{followups/outcome_history3/heldout_xlong/incomplete_commit/pct}, \val{followups/naive_history3/heldout_xlong/incomplete_commit/pct} and \val{followups/ma_history3/heldout_xlong/incomplete_commit/pct}). Most of these episodes run out of budget; the outcome-only agents with 8 actions also commit early with budget left in \val{followups/outcome_history/heldout_xlong/voluntary_incomplete_commit/pct} of episodes.

The agents' own rollouts show how much more often the long window meets observations it has not seen (\texttt{scripts/agent\_coverage.py}; an observation counts as unseen if it never occurs in the same checkpoint's greedy training-length rollouts). At 10--14, \val{agentcov/ma_history/heldout_xlong/unseen_rate/pct} of the monitorability-aware 8-action agents' decision points are unseen at the selected checkpoints and \val{agentcov/ma_history@final/heldout_xlong/unseen_rate/pct} at the last ones, against \val{agentcov/ma_history3/heldout_xlong/unseen_rate/pct} and \val{agentcov/ma_history3@final/heldout_xlong/unseen_rate/pct} with 3 actions and \val{agentcov/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/unseen_rate/pct} and \val{agentcov/ma_inv@final/heldout_xlong/unseen_rate/pct} for the invariant reference. At points with no slack left, \val{agentcov/ma_history/heldout_xlong/zero_slack_unseen_rate/pct} of the 8-action agents' observations are unseen (3 actions: \val{agentcov/ma_history3/heldout_xlong/zero_slack_unseen_rate/pct}; invariant: \val{agentcov/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/zero_slack_unseen_rate/pct}; selected checkpoints). Both windows over-check early in long chains (at the selected checkpoints the monitorability-aware agents check \val{agentcov/ma_history/heldout_xlong/uncued_early_rate/pct} of un-cued steps in the first half of the chain with 8 actions and \val{agentcov/ma_history3/heldout_xlong/uncued_early_rate/pct} with 3, against \val{agentcov/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/uncued_early_rate/pct}). Once no slack is left, any check or undo makes finishing impossible; the 8-action agents still check or undo at \val{agentcov/ma_history/heldout_xlong/zero_slack_spend_rate/pct} of such points (\val{agentcov/ma_history@final/heldout_xlong/zero_slack_spend_rate/pct} at the last checkpoint), the 3-action agents at \val{agentcov/ma_history3/heldout_xlong/zero_slack_spend_rate/pct}. Some of that spending comes in episodes that are already lost, for instance after a fault caught too late to repair, so it is not by itself the cause. For a greedy policy, spending at familiar observations is fixed by its training-length behaviour, so comparisons of familiar with unseen points within one agent say little; what differs between the windows is how often the critical points are unseen. The long window's failure is consistent with the coverage account of \S\ref{sec:length}, but this analysis does not show how the novelty turns into failure. A short memory of its own recent actions gives the learner what the pending bit gives, and for outcome-only agents more, without the hand-made state.

\paragraph{Paying at the repair step removes the detect-without-repair collapse.}
Moving the $0.25$ fault bonus from detection to the first correct re-execution of the located fault places a reward at the repair step itself. If the collapse of \S\ref{sec:norepair} is a credit-assignment failure, this should help. The test changes two things at once: it removes the $0.25$ detection payment and adds a repair payment, and we do not separate them. The cue bonus is still paid when a cued fault is caught. The ablation that removes the detection payment (together with the safe-check bonus) \emph{without} adding a repair payment (no check bonuses, \S\ref{sec:ablations}) still collapses in \val{ablations/no_check_bonuses/collapse_rate/pct} [\val{ablations/no_check_bonuses/collapse_rate/lo/pct}, \val{ablations/no_check_bonuses/collapse_rate/hi/pct}] of late checkpoints. The repair payment, not the missing detection payment, is therefore the likely active ingredient. Among late checkpoints (after 5{,}000 episodes), collapsed ones drop from \val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/pct} [\val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/lo/pct}, \val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/hi/pct}] to \val{followups/ma_repair/collapse_rate/pct} [\val{followups/ma_repair/collapse_rate/lo/pct}, \val{followups/ma_repair/collapse_rate/hi/pct}]. The mean absolute change between consecutive evaluations drops from \val{main/ma_inv/mean_abs_change} to \val{followups/ma_repair/mean_abs_change}. With the memory bit added as well, collapse disappears entirely (\val{followups/ma_memory_repair/collapse_rate/pct}). This is a reward-side remedy for the same failure; we did not try it with the other rewards. Its selected checkpoints come early (median episode \val{followups/ma_repair/selected_episode_median}, and \val{followups/ma_repair/selected_early}/10 within the first 5{,}000 episodes, which the collapse statistic excludes), and on long chains some of them still fail to repair: success after a detection at 10--14 is \val{followups/ma_repair/heldout_xlong/success_given_detected}, against \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success_given_detected} for the reference.

The benefit shows up in \emph{reliability} rather than peak performance. At selected checkpoints the repair-paid reward does not beat the original, because selection already discards collapsed checkpoints. It may even be slightly worse there: \val{diff/ma_repair-ma_inv/heldout_long/success} [\val{diff/ma_repair-ma_inv/heldout_long/success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_repair-ma_inv/heldout_long/success/hi}] at 7--9 ($p=\val{diff/ma_repair-ma_inv/heldout_long/success/p}$) and \val{diff/ma_repair-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success} [\val{diff/ma_repair-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_repair-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success/hi}] at 10--14, differences that 10 seeds do not resolve. At the last checkpoint it is better: $+$\val{diff/ma_repair@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success} [\val{diff/ma_repair@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_repair@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success/hi}] at 7--9 ($p=\val{diff/ma_repair@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success/p}$) and $+$\val{diff/ma_repair@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14 ($p=\val{diff/ma_repair@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_xlong/success/p}$).

The two last-checkpoint agents behave differently. The repair-paid agent checks less (\val{followups/ma_repair@final/heldout_long/verify_count} vs.\ \val{main/ma_inv@final/heldout_long/verify_count} checks per episode) and catches fewer faults (\val{followups/ma_repair@final/heldout_long/fault_detected} vs.\ \val{main/ma_inv@final/heldout_long/fault_detected}). But it almost always completes the repairs it starts (\val{followups/ma_repair@final/heldout_long/success_given_detected} vs.\ \val{main/ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success_given_detected}).
