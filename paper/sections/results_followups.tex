\subsection{Follow-up interventions}
\label{sec:followups}

The analyses above suggest three explanations, each with a direct test. We train each intervention with the monitorability-aware reward, invariant observations and 10 seeds, and compare it with the reference agent on paired episodes (Table~\ref{tab:followups}). These comparisons are exploratory and their $p$-values are not corrected for multiplicity.

\paragraph{Remaining-length information, added this way, makes the repair decision worse.}
Adding slack per remaining step, $\mathrm{clip}(\mathrm{slack}/(d-t), 0, 2)/2$, gives the agent the information that \S\ref{sec:residual} identifies as missing. Its values stay inside the interval seen in training, but many of them, and most of the observation vectors they form, never occur on training lengths. Observations at \val{coverage/ratio/heldout_long/unseen_rate/pct} of decision points at lengths 7--9 and \val{coverage/ratio/heldout_xlong/unseen_rate/pct} at 10--14 never appear in the training-length reference sample, against \val{coverage/invariant/heldout_long/unseen_rate/pct} and \val{coverage/invariant/heldout_xlong/unseen_rate/pct} for the invariant encoder. Held-out success falls to \val{followups/ma_ratio/heldout_long/success} at 7--9 and \val{followups/ma_ratio/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14 ($\val{diff/ma_ratio-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success}$, $p=\val{diff/ma_ratio-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success/p}$). Most of the loss comes from the repair decision rather than from allocation. (Half of these runs select a checkpoint from the first 5{,}000 episodes: median selected episode \val{followups/ma_ratio/selected_episode_median}, \val{followups/ma_ratio/selected_early}/10 within the burn-in; the numbers below partly describe immature policies.) Fault detection stays high (\val{followups/ma_ratio/heldout_xlong/fault_detected}), but repairs after a detection are completed in only \val{followups/ma_ratio/heldout_long/success_given_detected} of episodes at 7--9 and \val{followups/ma_ratio/heldout_xlong/success_given_detected} at 10--14, against \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_long/success_given_detected} and \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success_given_detected} for the reference. The extra feature destabilises the same fragile choice after an \textsc{unsafe} check that \S\ref{sec:norepair} studies, and it also costs \val{diff/ma_ratio-ma_inv/train_like/success} on training lengths. This run therefore does not show whether remaining-length information would help allocate the budget; it shows that adding it this way made the repair decision worse.

\paragraph{A memory bit removes most wasted \texttt{REVISE}s.}
Adding one bit that records whether an \textsc{unsafe} check has been seen removes the aliasing between repairs and no-ops. Wasted \texttt{REVISE}s at 10--14 fall from \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/nonrepair_revise_count} to \val{followups/ma_memory/heldout_xlong/nonrepair_revise_count} per episode. Training-length success rises by $+$\val{diff/ma_memory-ma_inv/train_like/success} (\val{diff/ma_memory-ma_inv/train_like/success/npos}/10 seeds, $p=\val{diff/ma_memory-ma_inv/train_like/success/p}$). Held-out success at selected checkpoints does not change resolvably ($p=\val{diff/ma_memory-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success/p}$ at 10--14), because the agent spends the recovered slack on more un-cued checks (\val{followups/ma_memory/heldout_xlong/verify_rate_cue0} vs.\ \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/verify_rate_cue0}). The allocation explanation of \S\ref{sec:residual} is therefore only partly confirmed: removing one source of waste was not enough. Checkpoint collapse is lower with the memory bit (\val{followups/ma_memory/collapse_rate/pct} vs.\ \val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/pct}), but the intervals overlap. Its last checkpoints are better than the reference's by $+$\val{diff/ma_memory@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success} at 7--9 ($p=\val{diff/ma_memory@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success/p}$) and $+$\val{diff/ma_memory@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14, a gain similar in size to the repair payment's below. The bit is not a test of the aliasing explanation of \S\ref{sec:norepair}: it stays set after a repair, so after continuing past a caught fault the agent still sees observations that also occur on track (\val{probe/ma_memory/aliased_rate/pct} of them, against \val{probe/ma_inv/aliased_rate/pct} without the bit). The repair-pending bit below is that test.

\paragraph{A repair-pending bit removes the failure under every reward.}
This bit is set by an \textsc{unsafe} check and cleared once the located step has been correctly re-executed, so it records exactly what the invariant observation hides; an agent that remembered its own checks and undos could compute it. We train it with all three rewards (Table~\ref{tab:pending}). Late checkpoints that detect faults without repairing them fall from \val{main/outcome_inv/collapse_norepair_rate/pct}, \val{main/naive_inv/collapse_norepair_rate/pct} and \val{main/ma_inv/collapse_norepair_rate/pct} (outcome-only, naive and monitorability-aware) to \val{followups/outcome_pending/collapse_norepair_rate/pct}, \val{followups/naive_pending/collapse_norepair_rate/pct} and \val{followups/ma_pending/collapse_norepair_rate/pct}. The greedy choice after an \textsc{unsafe} result becomes stable: the last checkpoints continue past a caught fault at \val{probe/outcome_pending@final/think_rate/pct}, \val{probe/naive_pending@final/think_rate/pct} and \val{probe/ma_pending@final/think_rate/pct} of such points. The bit does not make the values accurate (continuing is still overvalued by \val{probe/ma_pending/think_bias} for the monitorability-aware agents). Aliasing is therefore enough to make the choice unstable, but it is not the only route to stability: the repair payment below also stabilises it, although with that reward \val{probe/ma_repair/aliased_rate/pct} of the continuing-branch observations still occur on track.

The bit also changes how often outcome-only agents collapse at all. Outcome-only agents now collapse in \val{followups/outcome_pending/collapse_rate/pct} [\val{followups/outcome_pending/collapse_rate/lo/pct}, \val{followups/outcome_pending/collapse_rate/hi/pct}] of late checkpoints instead of \val{main/outcome_inv/collapse_rate/pct}, and fall into the low-detection regime in \val{followups/outcome_pending/collapse_lowdetect_rate/pct} instead of \val{main/outcome_inv/collapse_lowdetect_rate/pct}, presumably because checks pay off only once the repair that follows them is learned reliably. Part of what check payments buy in \S\ref{sec:learnability} is therefore compensation for this missing information. With the bit, the check payment shows up in training stability rather than in peak performance. At selected checkpoints the two rewards are indistinguishable on held-out lengths (monitorability-aware minus outcome-only: \val{diff/ma_pending-outcome_pending/heldout_long/success} [\val{diff/ma_pending-outcome_pending/heldout_long/success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_pending-outcome_pending/heldout_long/success/hi}] at 7--9 and \val{diff/ma_pending-outcome_pending/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14). But outcome-only agents still collapse more often (\val{followups/outcome_pending/collapse_rate/pct} of late checkpoints against \val{followups/ma_pending/collapse_rate/pct}), and their last checkpoints are much worse (\val{followups/outcome_pending@final/heldout_long/success} against \val{followups/ma_pending@final/heldout_long/success} at 7--9, $p=\val{diff/ma_pending@final-outcome_pending@final/heldout_long/success/p}$), already on training lengths (\val{followups/outcome_pending@final/train_like/success} against \val{followups/ma_pending@final/train_like/success}), so only part of that gap is a generalization deficit. With the bit, the monitorability-aware agents' last checkpoints are as good as their selected ones and better than the reference's last checkpoints (by \val{diff/ma_pending@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success} at 7--9, $p=\val{diff/ma_pending@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success/p}$).

At selected checkpoints the bit raises training-length success under every reward but lowers held-out success on long chains for the monitorability-aware agents (\val{diff/ma_pending-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success} [\val{diff/ma_pending-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_pending-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success/hi}] at 10--14) and, in point estimate, the naive ones (\val{diff/naive_pending-naive_inv/heldout_xlong/success} [\val{diff/naive_pending-naive_inv/heldout_xlong/success/lo}, \val{diff/naive_pending-naive_inv/heldout_xlong/success/hi}]). This is a selection effect rather than a cost of the bit. With the bit, training-length validation success saturates (median selected value \val{followups/ma_pending/selection_success_median}, against \val{main/ma_inv/selection_success_median}), so selection chooses among checkpoints that differ mainly in how they behave on long chains, and it tends to pick ones that check many un-cued steps (\val{followups/ma_pending/heldout_xlong/verify_rate_cue0} of un-cued decision points at 10--14, against \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/verify_rate_cue0}) and run short of slack. The last checkpoints of the same runs check un-cued steps less often (\val{followups/ma_pending@final/heldout_xlong/verify_rate_cue0}) and reach \ci{followups/ma_pending@final/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14, level with the cue-triggered heuristic (difference \val{diff/ma_pending@final-cue_triggered/heldout_xlong/success} [\val{diff/ma_pending@final-cue_triggered/heldout_xlong/success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_pending@final-cue_triggered/heldout_xlong/success/hi}]). Training-length selection is a poor guide once training-length performance saturates.

\paragraph{An agent that sees its recent actions learns the missing state, but not for longer chains.}
The pending bit is computed by the environment. To test whether a learner can infer it from its own history, we instead add the last 8 actions and check results to the invariant observation, one-hot coded and zero-padded at the start of an episode (Table~\ref{tab:pending}). On training lengths this removes collapse entirely under every reward (\val{followups/outcome_history/collapse_rate/pct}, \val{followups/naive_history/collapse_rate/pct} and \val{followups/ma_history/collapse_rate/pct} of late checkpoints for outcome-only, naive and monitorability-aware rewards) and gives the highest training-length success of any learned agent (\val{followups/outcome_history/train_like/success}, \val{followups/naive_history/train_like/success} and \val{followups/ma_history/train_like/success}). Even outcome-only agents now first exceed 0.5 by a median of episode \val{followups/outcome_history/first_above_half_median}, and their values after a caught fault are close to calibrated (continuing is overvalued by only \val{probe/outcome_history/think_bias}, against \val{probe/outcome_inv/think_bias} without the history). But the history makes the observation length-dependent again: the padding reveals how many actions have been taken early in an episode, and long chains produce windows that never occur in training (\val{coverage/history/heldout_xlong/unseen_rate/pct} of decision points at 10--14, against \val{coverage/invariant/heldout_xlong/unseen_rate/pct} for the invariant encoder). Held-out success falls to \val{followups/outcome_history/heldout_xlong/success}, \val{followups/naive_history/heldout_xlong/success} and \val{followups/ma_history/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14, and repairs after a detection often fail there (\val{followups/ma_history/heldout_xlong/success_given_detected} for the monitorability-aware agents). A learner with memory can thus learn the missing state from its own history; here it pays for it with the length generalization of \S\ref{sec:length}. A recurrent agent that summarises its history without exposing position might avoid that trade-off; we did not test one.

\paragraph{Paying at the repair step removes the detect-without-repair collapse.}
Moving the $0.25$ fault bonus from detection to the first correct re-execution of the located fault places a reward at the repair step itself. If the collapse of \S\ref{sec:norepair} is a credit-assignment failure, this should help. The test changes two things at once: it removes the $0.25$ detection payment and adds a repair payment, and we do not separate them. The cue bonus is still paid when a cued fault is caught. The ablation that removes the detection payment (together with the safe-check bonus) \emph{without} adding a repair payment (no check bonuses, \S\ref{sec:ablations}) still collapses in \val{ablations/no_check_bonuses/collapse_rate/pct} [\val{ablations/no_check_bonuses/collapse_rate/lo/pct}, \val{ablations/no_check_bonuses/collapse_rate/hi/pct}] of late checkpoints. The repair payment, not the missing detection payment, is therefore the likely active ingredient. Among late checkpoints (after 5{,}000 episodes), collapsed ones drop from \val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/pct} [\val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/lo/pct}, \val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/hi/pct}] to \val{followups/ma_repair/collapse_rate/pct} [\val{followups/ma_repair/collapse_rate/lo/pct}, \val{followups/ma_repair/collapse_rate/hi/pct}]. The mean absolute change between consecutive evaluations drops from \val{main/ma_inv/mean_abs_change} to \val{followups/ma_repair/mean_abs_change}. With the memory bit added as well, collapse disappears entirely (\val{followups/ma_memory_repair/collapse_rate/pct}). This is a reward-side remedy for the same failure; we did not try it with the other rewards. Its selected checkpoints come early (median episode \val{followups/ma_repair/selected_episode_median}, and \val{followups/ma_repair/selected_early}/10 within the first 5{,}000 episodes, which the collapse statistic excludes), and on long chains some of them still fail to repair: success after a detection at 10--14 is \val{followups/ma_repair/heldout_xlong/success_given_detected}, against \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success_given_detected} for the reference.

The benefit shows up in \emph{reliability} rather than peak performance. At selected checkpoints the repair-paid reward does not beat the original, because selection already discards collapsed checkpoints. It may even be slightly worse there: \val{diff/ma_repair-ma_inv/heldout_long/success} [\val{diff/ma_repair-ma_inv/heldout_long/success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_repair-ma_inv/heldout_long/success/hi}] at 7--9 ($p=\val{diff/ma_repair-ma_inv/heldout_long/success/p}$) and \val{diff/ma_repair-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success} [\val{diff/ma_repair-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_repair-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success/hi}] at 10--14, differences that 10 seeds do not resolve. At the last checkpoint it is better: $+$\val{diff/ma_repair@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success} [\val{diff/ma_repair@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_repair@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success/hi}] at 7--9 ($p=\val{diff/ma_repair@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success/p}$) and $+$\val{diff/ma_repair@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14 ($p=\val{diff/ma_repair@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_xlong/success/p}$).

The two last-checkpoint agents behave differently. The repair-paid agent checks less (\val{followups/ma_repair@final/heldout_long/verify_count} vs.\ \val{main/ma_inv@final/heldout_long/verify_count} checks per episode) and catches fewer faults (\val{followups/ma_repair@final/heldout_long/fault_detected} vs.\ \val{main/ma_inv@final/heldout_long/fault_detected}). But it almost always completes the repairs it starts (\val{followups/ma_repair@final/heldout_long/success_given_detected} vs.\ \val{main/ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success_given_detected}).
