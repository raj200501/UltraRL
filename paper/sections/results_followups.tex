\subsection{Follow-up interventions}
\label{sec:followups}

The analyses above suggest three explanations, each with a direct test. We train each intervention with the monitorability-aware reward, invariant observations and 10 seeds, and compare it with the reference agent on paired episodes (Table~\ref{tab:followups}).

\paragraph{Remaining-length information that is out of range does not help.}
Adding slack per remaining step, $\mathrm{clip}(\mathrm{slack}/(d-t), 0, 2)/2$, gives the agent the information that \S\ref{sec:residual} identifies as missing. Its feature values lie inside the training range, but in combinations that do not occur on training lengths. Observations at \val{coverage/ratio/heldout_long/unseen_rate/pct} of decision points at lengths 7--9 and \val{coverage/ratio/heldout_xlong/unseen_rate/pct} at 10--14 never appear in the training-length reference sample, against \val{coverage/invariant/heldout_long/unseen_rate/pct} and \val{coverage/invariant/heldout_xlong/unseen_rate/pct} for the invariant encoder. Held-out success falls to \val{followups/ma_ratio/heldout_long/success} at 7--9 and \val{followups/ma_ratio/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14 ($\val{diff/ma_ratio-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success}$, $p=\val{diff/ma_ratio-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success/p}$). Fault detection stays high (\val{followups/ma_ratio/heldout_xlong/fault_detected}), but repairs are rarely completed (\val{followups/ma_ratio/heldout_xlong/success_given_detected}). The extra feature also hurts in distribution, by $\val{diff/ma_ratio-ma_inv/train_like/success}$ on training lengths, so its effect cannot be attributed to out-of-range inputs alone. The result is consistent with the coverage explanation, and it shows that adding the missing information is not enough.

\paragraph{A memory bit removes most wasted \texttt{REVISE}s.}
Adding one bit that records whether an \textsc{unsafe} check has been seen removes the aliasing between repairs and no-ops. Wasted \texttt{REVISE}s at 10--14 fall from \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/nonrepair_revise_count} to \val{followups/ma_memory/heldout_xlong/nonrepair_revise_count} per episode. Training-length success rises by $+$\val{diff/ma_memory-ma_inv/train_like/success} (\val{diff/ma_memory-ma_inv/train_like/success/npos}/10 seeds, $p=\val{diff/ma_memory-ma_inv/train_like/success/p}$). Held-out success at selected checkpoints does not change resolvably ($p=\val{diff/ma_memory-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success/p}$ at 10--14), because the agent spends the recovered slack on more un-cued checks (\val{followups/ma_memory/heldout_xlong/verify_rate_cue0} vs.\ \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/verify_rate_cue0}). The allocation explanation of \S\ref{sec:residual} is therefore only partly confirmed: removing one source of waste was not enough. Checkpoint collapse is lower with the memory bit (\val{followups/ma_memory/collapse_rate/pct} vs.\ \val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/pct}), but the intervals overlap.

\paragraph{Paying at the repair step removes the detect-without-repair collapse.}
Moving the $0.25$ fault bonus from detection to the first correct re-execution of the located fault places a reward at the repair step itself. If the collapse of \S\ref{sec:norepair} is a credit-assignment failure, this should help. The test changes two things at once: it removes the $0.25$ detection payment and adds a repair payment, and we do not separate them. The cue bonus is still paid when a cued fault is caught. The ablation that removes the detection payment (together with the safe-check bonus) \emph{without} adding a repair payment (no check bonuses, \S\ref{sec:ablations}) still collapses in \val{ablations/no_check_bonuses/collapse_rate/pct} [\val{ablations/no_check_bonuses/collapse_rate/lo/pct}, \val{ablations/no_check_bonuses/collapse_rate/hi/pct}] of late checkpoints. The repair payment, not the missing detection payment, is therefore the likely active ingredient. Collapsed late checkpoints drop from \val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/pct} [\val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/lo/pct}, \val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/hi/pct}] to \val{followups/ma_repair/collapse_rate/pct} [\val{followups/ma_repair/collapse_rate/lo/pct}, \val{followups/ma_repair/collapse_rate/hi/pct}]. The mean absolute change between consecutive evaluations drops from \val{main/ma_inv/mean_abs_change} to \val{followups/ma_repair/mean_abs_change}. With the memory bit added as well, collapse disappears entirely (\val{followups/ma_memory_repair/collapse_rate/pct}).

The benefit shows up in \emph{reliability} rather than peak performance. At selected checkpoints the repair-paid reward does not beat the original (\val{diff/ma_repair-ma_inv/heldout_long/success} at 7--9, $p=\val{diff/ma_repair-ma_inv/heldout_long/success/p}$), because selection already discards collapsed checkpoints. At the last checkpoint it is better: $+$\val{diff/ma_repair@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success} [\val{diff/ma_repair@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_repair@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success/hi}] at 7--9 ($p=\val{diff/ma_repair@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success/p}$) and $+$\val{diff/ma_repair@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14 ($p=\val{diff/ma_repair@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_xlong/success/p}$).

The two last-checkpoint agents behave differently. The repair-paid agent checks less (\val{followups/ma_repair@final/heldout_long/verify_count} vs.\ \val{main/ma_inv@final/heldout_long/verify_count} checks per episode) and catches fewer faults (\val{followups/ma_repair@final/heldout_long/fault_detected} vs.\ \val{main/ma_inv@final/heldout_long/fault_detected}). But it almost always completes the repairs it starts (\val{followups/ma_repair@final/heldout_long/success_given_detected} vs.\ \val{main/ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success_given_detected}).
