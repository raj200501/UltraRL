\subsection{Follow-up interventions}
\label{sec:followups}

The analyses above suggest three explanations, each with a direct test. We train each intervention with the monitorability-aware reward, invariant observations and 10 seeds, and compare it with the reference agent on paired episodes (Table~\ref{tab:followups}). These comparisons are exploratory and their $p$-values are not corrected for multiplicity.

\paragraph{Remaining-length information, added this way, makes the repair decision worse.}
Adding slack per remaining step, $\mathrm{clip}(\mathrm{slack}/(d-t), 0, 2)/2$, gives the agent the information that \S\ref{sec:residual} identifies as missing. Its values stay inside the interval seen in training, but many of them, and most of the observation vectors they form, never occur on training lengths. Observations at \val{coverage/ratio/heldout_long/unseen_rate/pct} of decision points at lengths 7--9 and \val{coverage/ratio/heldout_xlong/unseen_rate/pct} at 10--14 never appear in the training-length reference sample, against \val{coverage/invariant/heldout_long/unseen_rate/pct} and \val{coverage/invariant/heldout_xlong/unseen_rate/pct} for the invariant encoder. Held-out success falls to \val{followups/ma_ratio/heldout_long/success} at 7--9 and \val{followups/ma_ratio/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14 ($\val{diff/ma_ratio-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success}$, $p=\val{diff/ma_ratio-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success/p}$). Most of the loss comes from the repair decision rather than from allocation. Fault detection stays high (\val{followups/ma_ratio/heldout_xlong/fault_detected}), but repairs after a detection are completed in only \val{followups/ma_ratio/heldout_long/success_given_detected} of episodes at 7--9 and \val{followups/ma_ratio/heldout_xlong/success_given_detected} at 10--14, against \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_long/success_given_detected} and \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success_given_detected} for the reference. The extra feature destabilises the same fragile choice after an \textsc{unsafe} check that \S\ref{sec:norepair} studies, and it also costs \val{diff/ma_ratio-ma_inv/train_like/success} on training lengths. This run therefore does not show whether remaining-length information would help allocate the budget; it shows that adding it this way made the repair decision worse.

\paragraph{A memory bit removes most wasted \texttt{REVISE}s.}
Adding one bit that records whether an \textsc{unsafe} check has been seen removes the aliasing between repairs and no-ops. Wasted \texttt{REVISE}s at 10--14 fall from \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/nonrepair_revise_count} to \val{followups/ma_memory/heldout_xlong/nonrepair_revise_count} per episode. Training-length success rises by $+$\val{diff/ma_memory-ma_inv/train_like/success} (\val{diff/ma_memory-ma_inv/train_like/success/npos}/10 seeds, $p=\val{diff/ma_memory-ma_inv/train_like/success/p}$). Held-out success at selected checkpoints does not change resolvably ($p=\val{diff/ma_memory-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success/p}$ at 10--14), because the agent spends the recovered slack on more un-cued checks (\val{followups/ma_memory/heldout_xlong/verify_rate_cue0} vs.\ \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/verify_rate_cue0}). The allocation explanation of \S\ref{sec:residual} is therefore only partly confirmed: removing one source of waste was not enough. Checkpoint collapse is lower with the memory bit (\val{followups/ma_memory/collapse_rate/pct} vs.\ \val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/pct}), but the intervals overlap. Its last checkpoints are better than the reference's by $+$\val{diff/ma_memory@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success} at 7--9 ($p=\val{diff/ma_memory@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success/p}$) and $+$\val{diff/ma_memory@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14, a gain similar in size to the repair payment's below. The bit is not a test of the aliasing explanation of \S\ref{sec:norepair}: it stays set after a repair, so after continuing past a caught fault the agent still sees observations that also occur on track (\val{probe/ma_memory/aliased_rate/pct} of them, against \val{probe/ma_inv/aliased_rate/pct} without the bit). The repair-pending bit below is that test.

\paragraph{A repair-pending bit removes the failure under every reward.}
This bit is set by an \textsc{unsafe} check and cleared once the located step has been correctly re-executed, so it records exactly what the invariant observation hides; an agent that remembered its own checks and undos could compute it. We train it with all three rewards (Table~\ref{tab:pending}). Late checkpoints that detect faults without repairing them fall from \val{main/outcome_inv/collapse_norepair_rate/pct}, \val{main/naive_inv/collapse_norepair_rate/pct} and \val{main/ma_inv/collapse_norepair_rate/pct} (outcome-only, naive and monitorability-aware) to \val{followups/outcome_pending/collapse_norepair_rate/pct}, \val{followups/naive_pending/collapse_norepair_rate/pct} and \val{followups/ma_pending/collapse_norepair_rate/pct}. The greedy choice after an \textsc{unsafe} result becomes stable: the last checkpoints continue past a caught fault at \val{probe/outcome_pending@final/think_rate/pct}, \val{probe/naive_pending@final/think_rate/pct} and \val{probe/ma_pending@final/think_rate/pct} of such points. The bit does not make the values accurate (continuing is still overrated by \val{probe/ma_pending/think_bias} for the monitorability-aware agents), so aliasing accounts for the instability but not for all of the miscalibration.

The bit also changes how often agents abandon checking. Outcome-only agents now collapse in \val{followups/outcome_pending/collapse_rate/pct} [\val{followups/outcome_pending/collapse_rate/lo/pct}, \val{followups/outcome_pending/collapse_rate/hi/pct}] of late checkpoints instead of \val{main/outcome_inv/collapse_rate/pct}, and stop checking in \val{followups/outcome_pending/collapse_nevercheck_rate/pct} instead of \val{main/outcome_inv/collapse_nevercheck_rate/pct}, presumably because checks pay off only once the repair that follows them is learned reliably. Part of what check payments buy in \S\ref{sec:learnability} is therefore compensation for this missing information. The payments still matter with the bit: outcome-only agents collapse more often than monitorability-aware ones (\val{followups/outcome_pending/collapse_rate/pct} against \val{followups/ma_pending/collapse_rate/pct}), and at the last checkpoint they generalize much worse (\val{followups/outcome_pending@final/heldout_long/success} against \val{followups/ma_pending@final/heldout_long/success} at 7--9, $p=\val{diff/ma_pending@final-outcome_pending@final/heldout_long/success/p}$). With the bit, the monitorability-aware agents' last checkpoints are as good as their selected ones and better than the reference's last checkpoints (by \val{diff/ma_pending@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success} at 7--9, $p=\val{diff/ma_pending@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success/p}$).

At selected checkpoints the bit raises training-length success under every reward, but on long chains it costs the process-rewarded agents: \val{diff/ma_pending-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success} [\val{diff/ma_pending-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_pending-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success/hi}] at 10--14 for the monitorability-aware reward. These agents check many more un-cued steps (at \val{followups/ma_pending/heldout_xlong/verify_rate_cue0} of un-cued decision points against \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/verify_rate_cue0}) and run short of slack for cued ones, the allocation problem of \S\ref{sec:residual} again.

\paragraph{Paying at the repair step removes the detect-without-repair collapse.}
Moving the $0.25$ fault bonus from detection to the first correct re-execution of the located fault places a reward at the repair step itself. If the collapse of \S\ref{sec:norepair} is a credit-assignment failure, this should help. The test changes two things at once: it removes the $0.25$ detection payment and adds a repair payment, and we do not separate them. The cue bonus is still paid when a cued fault is caught. The ablation that removes the detection payment (together with the safe-check bonus) \emph{without} adding a repair payment (no check bonuses, \S\ref{sec:ablations}) still collapses in \val{ablations/no_check_bonuses/collapse_rate/pct} [\val{ablations/no_check_bonuses/collapse_rate/lo/pct}, \val{ablations/no_check_bonuses/collapse_rate/hi/pct}] of late checkpoints. The repair payment, not the missing detection payment, is therefore the likely active ingredient. Among late checkpoints (after 5{,}000 episodes), collapsed ones drop from \val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/pct} [\val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/lo/pct}, \val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/hi/pct}] to \val{followups/ma_repair/collapse_rate/pct} [\val{followups/ma_repair/collapse_rate/lo/pct}, \val{followups/ma_repair/collapse_rate/hi/pct}]. The mean absolute change between consecutive evaluations drops from \val{main/ma_inv/mean_abs_change} to \val{followups/ma_repair/mean_abs_change}. With the memory bit added as well, collapse disappears entirely (\val{followups/ma_memory_repair/collapse_rate/pct}). This is a reward-side remedy for the same failure; we did not try it with the other rewards. Its selected checkpoints come early (median episode \val{followups/ma_repair/selected_episode_median}, and \val{followups/ma_repair/selected_early}/10 within the first 5{,}000 episodes, which the collapse statistic excludes), and on long chains some of them still fail to repair: success after a detection at 10--14 is \val{followups/ma_repair/heldout_xlong/success_given_detected}, against \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success_given_detected} for the reference.

The benefit shows up in \emph{reliability} rather than peak performance. At selected checkpoints the repair-paid reward does not beat the original, because selection already discards collapsed checkpoints. It may even be slightly worse there: \val{diff/ma_repair-ma_inv/heldout_long/success} [\val{diff/ma_repair-ma_inv/heldout_long/success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_repair-ma_inv/heldout_long/success/hi}] at 7--9 ($p=\val{diff/ma_repair-ma_inv/heldout_long/success/p}$) and \val{diff/ma_repair-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success} [\val{diff/ma_repair-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_repair-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success/hi}] at 10--14, differences that 10 seeds do not resolve. At the last checkpoint it is better: $+$\val{diff/ma_repair@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success} [\val{diff/ma_repair@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_repair@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success/hi}] at 7--9 ($p=\val{diff/ma_repair@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success/p}$) and $+$\val{diff/ma_repair@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14 ($p=\val{diff/ma_repair@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_xlong/success/p}$).

The two last-checkpoint agents behave differently. The repair-paid agent checks less (\val{followups/ma_repair@final/heldout_long/verify_count} vs.\ \val{main/ma_inv@final/heldout_long/verify_count} checks per episode) and catches fewer faults (\val{followups/ma_repair@final/heldout_long/fault_detected} vs.\ \val{main/ma_inv@final/heldout_long/fault_detected}). But it almost always completes the repairs it starts (\val{followups/ma_repair@final/heldout_long/success_given_detected} vs.\ \val{main/ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success_given_detected}).
