\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What rewarding checks buys.}
Paying for informative checks did not unlock behaviour that outcome reward can never reach: with a suitable observation, outcome-only agents also find checking policies. What it bought was speed and retention. Outcome-only agents found checking policies late, mostly lost them again, and did not catch up with twice the training. Agents under the naive process reward collapsed less often but still in most late checkpoints. Its step-correctness bonus alone, the term closest to a process reward model, bought more stability the larger it was, and not through the undo-and-redo loop that a repeatable step bonus invites; at its largest it prevented collapse about as well as a substantial check payment, but its checkpoints plateaued lower, with no resolved gain at selected checkpoints. In the main and sensitivity experiments, agents paid substantially for checks never fell into checking that misses faults (some follow-up and ablation variants did, in at most \val{extremes/check_paying/max/collapse_lowdetect_rate/pct} of late checkpoints, apart from the ablation without \texttt{REVISE}, which cannot repair). Part of this benefit, though, compensates for information the invariant observation hides (see below). The contrast between outcome-only and substantial payment held under every learner setting we tried; the held-out benefit of the token payment did not. On training lengths, most of that benefit came from paying for checks at all, not from any specific term (\S\ref{sec:ablations}). What decided whether the learned checking worked on long chains was its \emph{allocation}. The gap term, meant to keep traces diagnosable, rewards any check that closes an unchecked stretch. At selected checkpoints, agents without the cue terms to counterbalance it over-checked and ran out of budget on long chains; this interaction does not reproduce at the last checkpoint, and we did not isolate the gap term's part in it. Step-level rewards shape not only whether an agent checks but how it spends a shared budget, and in this testbed that mattered more for generalization than whether checking was learned at all.

\paragraph{What the observation records matters as much as what the reward pays.}
Agents under every reward drifted into policies that catch faults and then fail to repair them. This is a learning failure, not specification gaming, because repairing is worth far more than any detection bonus. A single bit recording that a correction is still pending removed the failure under every reward and made the greedy choice stable, although the learned values stayed miscalibrated. Under the monitorability-aware reward, a reward-side change (paying the fault bonus at the repair step rather than at detection) also stabilised the choice, and an observation-side change (a memory of having seen a fault) helped less clearly. For learners with limited state, the lesson is that a reward for noticing a problem does little if the state does not record that the problem is still open; either the state should carry it or the reward should pay for completing the correction. Language-model reasoners condition on their whole trace, so for them the question is whether such state is learned from history. Agents that saw only their last 3 actions learned it: at most \val{followups/naive_history3/collapse_rate/pct} of their late checkpoints collapsed under any reward, and their last checkpoints kept most of their performance on longer chains. Agents that saw their last 8 actions also stopped collapsing but generalized poorly. On training lengths that window holds almost the whole episode; on long chains it produces windows the policy has not seen, and at those windows the agents spent slack they no longer had (\S\ref{sec:followups}). More history is therefore not simply better: a memory whose contents grow with the episode reintroduces the novelty that the invariant encoder removed. The bit changed the case for check payments only in part. It narrowed their advantage in collapse, but with the bit, outcome-only late checkpoints still trailed the check-paying agents' by about as much as without it (\val{followups/outcome_pending/late_success} against \val{followups/ma_pending/late_success} mean training-length success). Recent actions narrowed that gap further, most of the way already with 3 actions (\val{followups/outcome_history3/late_success} against \val{followups/ma_history3/late_success}) and almost completely with 8 (\val{followups/outcome_history/late_success} against \val{followups/ma_history/late_success}). Near the ceiling, though, absolute gaps are compressed: even with 8 actions, outcome-only late checkpoints failed \val{ratio/history/late_failure} times as often as check-paid ones, about the same ratio as without any addition (\val{ratio/inv/late_failure}). Check payments thus helped under every observation we tried, by less in absolute terms the more the observation carried.

\paragraph{What rewarding checks does not buy.}
Process rewards did not fix length generalization. With the original encoder's length-dependent features, the best reward still fell below the never-check baseline on long chains, where every observation was novel to the policy. Removing only the two position features was not enough. Two kinds of features plausibly cause trouble: features whose mapping depends on the chain length (the budget fraction and the slack scaled by the total budget), and features with a fixed meaning that take values on long chains never seen in training (the gap normalised by a fixed constant, and, in the follow-ups, slack per remaining step). The invariant encoder avoids the first kind and caps the second (which also changes the gap feature on training lengths). Our encoder control removes only the position features and keeps both kinds, so we have not separated them. A feature that adds the missing remaining-length information did not help either; it mainly destabilised the repair decision. For process-supervised reasoning models, this suggests that ``learned to verify on training problems'' and ``will verify appropriately on longer problems'' are separate claims, to be evaluated separately. Length-dependent features are a plausible way for the second to fail while the first holds.

\paragraph{Evaluation lessons.}
Two problems in the earlier version of this study pushed its conclusion toward a false negative: a representation confound, and last-checkpoint evaluation of unstable policies. A third, a localization metric that credited unchecked faults, inflated diagnostic scores without changing the main comparison. None of the three was visible in the headline numbers. We recommend four practices for RL on reasoning-like tasks:
\begin{itemize}
  \item report learning curves and per-seed variability;
  \item select checkpoints only on in-distribution validation;
  \item measure how much of a held-out set produces inputs the policy never saw in training;
  \item test whether out-of-distribution failures move when the representation changes but the reward does not.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The environment is deliberately minimal. It has at most one fault, checks are exact, the cue is given rather than learned, and the agent is a small MLP rather than a language model. The ``monitorability-aware'' reward is a proxy with no monitor behind it. Process rewards use privileged step correctness, standing in for an idealised process reward model. The terms that help on long chains, the cue terms, encode the same knowledge as the cue-triggered heuristic. Their benefit is therefore evidence that well-designed shaping helps, not that a generic step-correctness reward model would: the naive process reward and its step term, which are closer to one, made training more stable but did not resolvably raise the success of the selected checkpoints, even with a step bonus seven times the original. A step bonus that large also makes redoing correct steps profitable unless it is paid once per step. The exact planner is proven optimal only among policies that never go back to check an earlier step; we verified that this restriction does not bind on chains of up to 7 steps, not beyond. We study one learner (Double DQN) with a small sensitivity sweep; policy-gradient or recurrent agents might be more stable or allocate their budget differently. The encoders differ in feature scaling as well as in length dependence. The coverage statistic counts exact matches, so it measures novelty, not distance: many unseen held-out observations of the no-position control lie close to training observations. It is consistent with the held-out differences but does not by itself explain them, nor why the absolute encoders also learn worse on training lengths. With 10 seeds, differences of a few points are not resolved. Only a family of six headline comparisons, chosen after the results, is corrected for multiplicity; the remaining $p$-values should be read as exploratory. Finally, at selected checkpoints no encoder we tried closes the gap to the cue-triggered heuristic on the longest chains; only the last checkpoints of the monitorability-aware agents with the repair-pending bit or the 3-action history come within an unresolved difference of it (\val{diff/ma_pending@final-cue_triggered/heldout_xlong/success} and \val{diff/ma_history3@final-cue_triggered/heldout_xlong/success}; comparisons made after seeing the results).

\section{Conclusion}
In a small budgeted-verification testbed, rewarding informative checks helped agents find checking policies sooner and keep them. Whether the learned checking carried over to longer chains depended mostly on the observation encoder, and on whether the reward kept checking frugal, more than on whether checking was learned at all. A failure shared by every reward, catching faults without repairing them, disappeared under every reward once the observation recorded whether a caught fault was still open, or showed the agent its last few actions. Correcting a representation confound turned an apparent negative result about process-rewarded RL into a positive but qualified one. We hope the testbed is useful for probing how process supervision shapes \emph{when} learned reasoners check their work.
