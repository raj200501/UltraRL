\section{Environment Details}
\label{app:env}

\paragraph{Transitions.}
Given the current true value $v$, an operation type is drawn uniformly from those available, and then its operand uniformly: \texttt{add}~$k$ with $k \in \{1,\dots,4\}$; \texttt{sub}~$k$ with $k \in \{1,\dots,\min(4, v-1)\}$ if $v > 2$; and \texttt{mul}~$k$ with $k \in \{2,3\}$ if $|v| \le 16$. The corrupted version of \texttt{add}/\texttt{sub}~$k$ uses $k{+}1$, and that of \texttt{mul}~$k$ is \texttt{add}~$k$. If the corrupted operation happens to give the same value, it is replaced by \texttt{add}~$k{+}2$. A corrupted step therefore always changes the value (unit-tested).

\paragraph{Fault prior.}
Conditional on a fault, its position $h \in \{0,\dots,d-1\}$ has weight $1 + 5\,(1 - |h - c| / \max(1, c))$ with $c = (d-1)/2$, i.e.\ it peaks in the middle of the chain.

\paragraph{Cue selectivity metric.}
At every decision point where the current step $t > 0$ has not been checked and \texttt{VERIFY} is available, we record the cue $c_t$ and whether the agent chose \texttt{VERIFY}. $P(\texttt{VERIFY} \mid c)$ pools these counts over all evaluation episodes of a seed.

\paragraph{Validation against the original implementation.}
The environment was rewritten for this version. \texttt{make equivalence} (\texttt{scripts/check\_legacy\_equivalence.py}) compares it with the original implementation, vendored from commit \texttt{81ec8cb} into \texttt{scripts/legacy/} with formatting changes only, by playing identical random action sequences in both. Over 86{,}112 steps from 18{,}000 episodes, covering all reward schemes and both budget-penalty settings, the two agree exactly on transitions, masks, rewards, termination and absolute observations. The only difference is the observation returned after the terminal \texttt{COMMIT}, which no learner uses.

A training run takes about four minutes on one CPU core. The main experiment (\val{runs/main} runs), the ablations (\val{runs/ablations/own} runs; the full-reward condition is shared with the main experiment), the sensitivity study (\val{runs/sensitivity} runs, ten of them twice as long), the follow-ups (\val{runs/followups} runs), the encoder control (\val{runs/encoders} runs) and the step-bonus sweep (\val{runs/process} runs) are \val{runs/distinct} distinct runs, \val{runs/core_hours} CPU-hours in total (\val{runs/untimed} early runs have no recorded time and are counted at the mean of runs of the same length), about \val{runs/hours_on_4_cores} hours on a 4-core machine. No GPU is used. Training is deterministic on CPU given a seed; \texttt{make reproduce} retrains everything into a fresh directory (\val{runs/saved} runs, since the shared condition is retrained there) and checks every checkpoint bitwise against the saved ones. Bitwise identity requires the pinned versions in \texttt{requirements-lock.txt} and the same CPU instruction set: with AVX2 or without vector extensions torch's kernels round differently, which changes every training trajectory. The platform (Python, torch and numpy versions, CPU capability, BLAS) is recorded in \texttt{run\_info.json} from the step-bonus sweep on (x86-64 Linux, AVX-512, MKL); the other experiments predate that record. \texttt{make spot-check} (about 5 minutes) retrains one run from each experiment and compares it bitwise; on an AVX-512 machine all six reproduce exactly. On other hardware, \texttt{scripts/compare\_summaries.py} compares retrained and saved success per method, paired by seed, and checks whether the headline comparisons keep their sign and resolution.

\section{Additional Follow-up Results}
\label{app:followups}

\paragraph{Slack per remaining step.}
Adding slack per remaining step, $\mathrm{clip}(\mathrm{slack}/(d-t), 0, 2)/2$, gives the agent the information that \S\ref{sec:residual} identifies as missing. Its values stay inside the interval seen in training, but many of them, and most of the observation vectors they form, never occur on training lengths. Observations at \val{coverage/ratio/heldout_long/unseen_rate/pct} of decision points at lengths 7--9 and \val{coverage/ratio/heldout_xlong/unseen_rate/pct} at 10--14 never appear in the training-length reference sample, against \val{coverage/invariant/heldout_long/unseen_rate/pct} and \val{coverage/invariant/heldout_xlong/unseen_rate/pct} for the invariant encoder. Held-out success falls to \val{followups/ma_ratio/heldout_long/success} at 7--9 and \val{followups/ma_ratio/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14 ($\val{diff/ma_ratio-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success}$, $p=\val{diff/ma_ratio-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success/p}$). Most of the loss comes from the repair decision rather than from allocation. (Half of these runs select a checkpoint from the first 5{,}000 episodes: median selected episode \val{followups/ma_ratio/selected_episode_median}, \val{followups/ma_ratio/selected_early}/10 within the burn-in; the numbers below partly describe immature policies.) Fault detection stays high (\val{followups/ma_ratio/heldout_xlong/fault_detected}), but repairs after a detection are completed in only \val{followups/ma_ratio/heldout_long/success_given_detected} of episodes at 7--9 and \val{followups/ma_ratio/heldout_xlong/success_given_detected} at 10--14, against \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_long/success_given_detected} and \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success_given_detected} for the reference. The extra feature destabilises the same fragile choice after an \textsc{unsafe} check that \S\ref{sec:norepair} studies, and it also costs \val{diff/ma_ratio-ma_inv/train_like/success} on training lengths. This run therefore does not show whether remaining-length information would help allocate the budget; it shows that adding it this way made the repair decision worse.

\paragraph{Checkpoint selection with the repair-pending bit.}
At selected checkpoints the bit raises training-length success under every reward but lowers held-out success on long chains for the monitorability-aware agents (\val{diff/ma_pending-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success} [\val{diff/ma_pending-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_pending-ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success/hi}] at 10--14) and, in point estimate, the naive ones (\val{diff/naive_pending-naive_inv/heldout_xlong/success} [\val{diff/naive_pending-naive_inv/heldout_xlong/success/lo}, \val{diff/naive_pending-naive_inv/heldout_xlong/success/hi}]). For the monitorability-aware agents this is most likely a selection effect rather than a cost of the bit (for the naive agents it is not: their last checkpoints are no better). Of these selected checkpoints, \val{followups/ma_pending/selected_early}/10 come from the first 5{,}000 episodes. With the bit, training-length validation success saturates (median selected value \val{followups/ma_pending/selection_success_median}, against \val{main/ma_inv/selection_success_median}), so selection chooses among checkpoints that differ mainly in how they behave on long chains, and it tends to pick ones that check many un-cued steps (\val{followups/ma_pending/heldout_xlong/verify_rate_cue0} of un-cued decision points at 10--14, against \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/verify_rate_cue0}) and run short of slack. The last checkpoints of the same runs check un-cued steps less often (\val{followups/ma_pending@final/heldout_xlong/verify_rate_cue0}) and reach \ci{followups/ma_pending@final/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14, an unresolved difference from the cue-triggered heuristic of \val{diff/ma_pending@final-cue_triggered/heldout_xlong/success} [\val{diff/ma_pending@final-cue_triggered/heldout_xlong/success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_pending@final-cue_triggered/heldout_xlong/success/hi}] (a comparison we made after seeing the long-chain results). Training-length selection is a poor guide once training-length performance saturates; it also flatters the reference agent's selected numbers. Compared like for like, last checkpoint against last checkpoint, the bit helps by $+$\val{diff/ma_pending@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success} at 7--9 and $+$\val{diff/ma_pending@final-ma_inv@final/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14.

\section{Changes from the Earlier Version}
\label{app:changes}

The first public version of this repository (commit \texttt{81ec8cb}) reported that monitorability-aware RL reached 0.60 success on training lengths but only 0.11 on lengths 7--9, and concluded that the learned checking behaviour was brittle under length shift. This version changes the following.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Representation control.} The original observation contained absolute position and depth features. We add a length-invariant encoder and compare the two directly (\S\ref{sec:length}). The encoder, not the reward, explains most of the length-generalization failure.
  \item \textbf{Training length and checkpoint selection.} Runs were trained for 5{,}000 episodes, while learning curves were still rising, and evaluated at the last checkpoint. We train for 20{,}000 episodes, select checkpoints on training-length validation only, and report last-checkpoint results as well (Appendix~\ref{app:final}).
  \item \textbf{Seed overlap.} Training episode seeds were \texttt{seed}$\cdot 10^5 + i$ and evaluation seeds \texttt{seed}$\cdot 10^6 + i$, which coincide for seed~0: 299 of seed~0's 300 training-length evaluation episodes were exact copies of training episodes. Because policies observe only depth, position, budget, cue and check results, an episode is determined (for a deterministic policy) by its depth, fault position and cue vector, and on training lengths every such configuration recurs many times in training anyway. The overlap therefore could not bias the reported numbers; we fixed it for hygiene by using independent random-number streams, and tests check that evaluation draws from its own stream.
  \item \textbf{Localization metric.} The original ``failed-episode localization score'' gave full credit when the fault was on the last step and the agent committed without checking anything, and partial credit for any commit at or after the fault step; as a result the never-check policy scored 0.07. It inflated every method's diagnostic score without changing the main comparison. We replace it with fault detection, the probability that a check caught the fault, and add repair completion.
  \item \textbf{Stronger baselines and statistics.} We add a budget-aware heuristic tuned on training lengths, use 10 seeds instead of 5, and report $t$-intervals, paired differences and permutation tests instead of standard errors.
  \item \textbf{Learner details.} The replay buffer now samples minibatches with replacement (the original sampled without replacement), and the original's per-step \texttt{invalid\_action\_penalty} was dropped because masked agents never take invalid actions. Neither affects the environment's dynamics or rewards (Appendix~\ref{app:env}).
  \item \textbf{Heuristic definitions.} The original heuristics committed as soon as the last step was executed, so they never checked the final step, and the cue-triggered heuristic re-checked a step after repairing it. Ours check the final step and never re-check. On the same evaluation episodes this raises cue-triggered success at lengths 3--6 from 0.82 to \val{main/cue_triggered/train_like/success} (almost entirely from the final-step check) and verify-every-step success from 0.57 to \val{main/verify_every_step/train_like/success}. Learned agents are unaffected.
\end{enumerate}
