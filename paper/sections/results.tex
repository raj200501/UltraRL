\subsection{Paying for checks helps agents keep checking}
\label{sec:learnability}

Never checking succeeds exactly when no fault is present, which happens 20\% of the time. Any gain above that requires the agent to discover a three-step pattern: check a suspicious step, see \textsc{unsafe}, then undo and redo.

\paragraph{Absolute observations.}
Outcome-only agents rarely acquire this pattern. Their selected checkpoints reach \ci{main/outcome_abs/train_like/success} on training lengths, and no seed's validation success ever exceeds 0.5 (\val{main/outcome_abs/seeds_peak_above_half}/10). The monitorability-aware reward reaches \ci{main/ma_abs/train_like/success}: a paired difference of $+$\val{diff/ma_abs-outcome_abs/train_like/success} [\val{diff/ma_abs-outcome_abs/train_like/success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_abs-outcome_abs/train_like/success/hi}] in \val{diff/ma_abs-outcome_abs/train_like/success/npos}/10 seeds (Holm-adjusted $p=\val{diff/ma_abs-outcome_abs/train_like/success/pholm}$).

\paragraph{Invariant observations.}
Outcome-only agents \emph{do} find checking policies: some validation checkpoint exceeds 0.5 in \val{main/outcome_inv/seeds_peak_above_half}/10 seeds. They rarely keep them. After the first 5{,}000 episodes, \val{main/outcome_inv/collapse_rate/pct} [\val{main/outcome_inv/collapse_rate/lo/pct}, \val{main/outcome_inv/collapse_rate/hi/pct}] of their greedy checkpoints have collapsed to success $\le 0.35$ (Figure~\ref{fig:curves}). For the monitorability-aware reward the figure is \val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/pct} [\val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/lo/pct}, \val{main/ma_inv/collapse_rate/hi/pct}]; intervals are over seeds. Naive process reward also pays for informative checks, but only a token $0.02$. It helps partially: its late checkpoints collapse in \val{main/naive_inv/collapse_rate/pct} [\val{main/naive_inv/collapse_rate/lo/pct}, \val{main/naive_inv/collapse_rate/hi/pct}] of cases, and have nearly stopped checking in \val{main/naive_inv/collapse_nevercheck_rate/pct} [\val{main/naive_inv/collapse_nevercheck_rate/lo/pct}, \val{main/naive_inv/collapse_nevercheck_rate/hi/pct}], against \val{main/outcome_inv/collapse_nevercheck_rate/pct} for outcome-only. The monitorability-aware reward pays $0.20$--$0.43$ for an informative check of a cued step and $0.25$ for catching an un-cued fault (an un-cued check that finds nothing nets zero). The two process rewards differ in more than the size of this payment (the naive reward also pays for correct steps and has no cue, gap or waste terms), so the difference between them cannot be attributed to payment size alone. The two kinds of collapse also differ. Most collapsed outcome-only checkpoints have nearly stopped checking: fault detection is below 0.2 in \val{main/outcome_inv/collapse_nevercheck_rate/pct} of all late checkpoints. No collapsed monitorability-aware checkpoint has (\val{main/ma_inv/collapse_nevercheck_rate/pct}); those checkpoints still catch faults but fail to repair them (\S\ref{sec:norepair}).

At selected checkpoints, the monitorability-aware reward beats outcome-only by $+$\val{diff/ma_inv-outcome_inv/train_like/success} [\val{diff/ma_inv-outcome_inv/train_like/success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_inv-outcome_inv/train_like/success/hi}] on training lengths (Holm-adjusted $p=\val{diff/ma_inv-outcome_inv/train_like/success/pholm}$). Its advantage over naive process reward is smaller: $+$\val{diff/ma_inv-naive_inv/train_like/success} [\val{diff/ma_inv-naive_inv/train_like/success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_inv-naive_inv/train_like/success/hi}] ($p=\val{diff/ma_inv-naive_inv/train_like/success/p}$) on training lengths and $+$\val{diff/ma_inv-naive_inv/heldout_long/success} ($p=\val{diff/ma_inv-naive_inv/heldout_long/success/p}$) at 7--9.

The process reward that most resembles a learned process reward model, naive process reward, which pays for correct steps, is not reliably better than outcome-only on held-out lengths (\S\ref{sec:length}). What helps is paying for \emph{checks} (\S\ref{sec:ablations}), and the monitorability-aware reward pays most for the checks a hand-coded cue-triggered policy makes. Its training-length success of \ci{main/ma_inv/train_like/success} lies between that heuristic (\val{main/cue_triggered/train_like/success}) and the tuned budget-aware heuristic (\val{main/budget_aware_1/train_like/success}).

\subsection{A shared failure mode: detecting without repairing}
\label{sec:norepair}

A checkpoint can fail even though it still checks. It catches the fault with an \textsc{unsafe} result and then does not complete the repair. Late checkpoints that collapse in this way, with success $\le 0.35$ but at least half of all faults caught, occur under every reward: \val{main/outcome_inv/collapse_norepair_rate/pct} of late checkpoints for outcome-only, \val{main/naive_inv/collapse_norepair_rate/pct} for naive process and \val{main/ma_inv/collapse_norepair_rate/pct} for monitorability-aware rewards, all with invariant observations. The effect is also visible in evaluated agents (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:behaviour}). Even on training lengths, where no input is out of range, the last checkpoints of the monitorability-aware agents complete the repair after a caught fault in only \val{main/ma_abs@final/train_like/success_given_detected/pct} (absolute features) and \val{main/ma_inv@final/train_like/success_given_detected/pct} (invariant features) of episodes. The cue-triggered and budget-aware heuristics always complete it (\val{main/cue_triggered/train_like/success_given_detected/pct}).

This is a learning failure, not reward hacking in the sense of a policy that scores well on the specified reward but not on the intended one~\citep{krakovna2020specification,skalse2022hacking}. Under every reward, completing the repair is worth far more than any detection bonus: success pays $+1$ and failure $-1.2$, against at most $0.43$ for catching a cued fault. A detect-without-repair policy therefore earns less return than a repairing one even under the monitorability-aware reward.

\paragraph{The learned values barely separate repairing from continuing.}
We probe each checkpoint at the decision points right after an \textsc{unsafe} result, on training-length evaluation episodes (\texttt{scripts/q\_probe.py}). For each such point we compare the learned values of \texttt{REVISE} and \texttt{THINK} with their true discounted returns under the same greedy policy, computed exactly by playing both actions out from a copy of the environment. For the selected monitorability-aware checkpoints, the median learned gap $Q(\texttt{REVISE}) - Q(\texttt{THINK})$ is \val{probe/ma_inv/q_gap}, while the median true gap is \val{probe/ma_inv/true_gap}. Continuing past the fault is valued at \val{probe/ma_inv/q_think} on average, against a true return of \val{probe/ma_inv/return_think}. The greedy choice is right at almost every such point (\texttt{THINK} in \val{probe/ma_inv/think_rate/pct}), but by a margin small enough that ordinary updates can reverse it: the last checkpoints choose \texttt{THINK} in \val{probe/ma_inv@final/think_rate/pct} of these points. Outcome-only and naive agents show the same pattern (learned gaps of \val{probe/outcome_inv/q_gap} and \val{probe/naive_inv/q_gap}).

A likely reason is state aliasing~\citep{singh1994learning}. The invariant observation does not record that a fault was found, so after continuing past it the agent sees observations that also occur when its running value is correct: \val{probe/ma_inv/aliased_rate/pct} of the observations on the continuing branch also occur on track in the same agent's rollouts. Bootstrapped values of continuing are pulled toward on-track values, and the failure that follows shows up only as a slightly lower average outcome for the shared observations. Delayed credit assignment, with the cost of not repairing visible only at \texttt{COMMIT}, may also contribute. \S\ref{sec:followups} tests both explanations: a memory bit that records a located fault, and a fault bonus paid at the repair step.

\subsection{Length generalization is mainly a representation problem}
\label{sec:length}

Figure~\ref{fig:length} separates the effect of the reward from the effect of the observation encoding. With the original absolute encoder, success falls sharply on longer chains for every reward. On lengths 10--14 all three absolute-encoder agents are \emph{worse than never checking}: \val{main/outcome_abs/heldout_xlong/success}, \val{main/naive_abs/heldout_xlong/success} and \val{main/ma_abs/heldout_xlong/success}, versus \val{main/always_think/heldout_xlong/success}. On lengths 10--14 they mostly fail in one specific way: they commit before executing a single step, in \val{main/ma_abs/heldout_xlong/commit_at_start/pct} of monitorability-aware episodes (only \val{main/ma_abs/heldout_long/commit_at_start/pct} at 7--9, where their failures are more varied). The depth feature $d/9$ exceeds 1 on these chains, and the policy has never seen such an input.

More generally, every observation the absolute encoder produces on held-out lengths is new. Across rollouts of the hand-coded policies, \val{coverage/absolute/heldout_long/unseen_rate/pct} of decision points at lengths 7--9 have an observation vector that does not occur in a large reference sample from lengths 3--6. For the invariant encoder the figure is \val{coverage/invariant/heldout_long/unseen_rate/pct} at 7--9 and \val{coverage/invariant/heldout_xlong/unseen_rate/pct} at 10--14.

Changing only the observation encoder removes most of the failure. The monitorability-aware agent with invariant features keeps \ci{main/ma_inv/heldout_long/success} at lengths 7--9 and \ci{main/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14. These are paired improvements over the absolute encoder of $+$\val{diff/ma_inv-ma_abs/heldout_long/success} [\val{diff/ma_inv-ma_abs/heldout_long/success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_inv-ma_abs/heldout_long/success/hi}] and $+$\val{diff/ma_inv-ma_abs/heldout_xlong/success} [\val{diff/ma_inv-ma_abs/heldout_xlong/success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_inv-ma_abs/heldout_xlong/success/hi}], in \val{diff/ma_inv-ma_abs/heldout_xlong/success/npos}/10 seeds (Holm-adjusted $p=\val{diff/ma_inv-ma_abs/heldout_xlong/success/pholm}$). At lengths 7--9 its difference from the cue-triggered heuristic is \val{diff/ma_inv-cue_triggered/heldout_long/success} [\val{diff/ma_inv-cue_triggered/heldout_long/success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_inv-cue_triggered/heldout_long/success/hi}]. This is not significant ($p=\val{diff/ma_inv-cue_triggered/heldout_long/success/p}$), but it does not demonstrate equivalence either. Both remain below the exact planner (\val{main/optimal/heldout_long/success}).

\paragraph{It is not only the position features.}
The absolute encoder differs from the invariant one in more than position. Its budget fraction, slack normalisation and gap normalisation also depend on chain length. A control encoder keeps all of these but drops the two position features $t/9$ and $d/9$ (Table~\ref{tab:encoders}). It no longer commits at the start, but it generalizes almost as badly as the full absolute encoder: \ci{encoders/ma_abs_nopos/heldout_long/success} at 7--9 and \ci{encoders/ma_abs_nopos/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14. Its observations are also mostly unseen on held-out lengths (\val{coverage/absolute_nopos/heldout_xlong/unseen_rate/pct} at 10--14). What matters is whether \emph{any} input changes meaning with length, not the position features specifically.

\subsection{What the agents learn: checking cued steps}
\label{sec:selectivity}

Figure~\ref{fig:selectivity} shows how often each agent checks at a decision point whose current step is unchecked, split by whether that step's cue fired. With invariant features all three rewards produce cue-conditional checking. What distinguishes the monitorability-aware reward is \emph{coverage} of cued steps, not avoidance of un-cued ones. It checks at \val{main/ma_inv/train_like/verify_rate_cue1} of cued decision points versus \val{main/outcome_inv/train_like/verify_rate_cue1} for outcome-only, while checking un-cued ones at a similar rate (\val{main/ma_inv/train_like/verify_rate_cue0} vs.\ \val{main/outcome_inv/train_like/verify_rate_cue0}). Outcome-only agents with absolute features barely condition on the cue at all (\val{main/outcome_abs/train_like/verify_rate_cue1} vs.\ \val{main/outcome_abs/train_like/verify_rate_cue0}). With absolute features, the monitorability-aware agent's cue coverage decays on held-out lengths (\val{main/ma_abs/heldout_long/verify_rate_cue1} at 7--9). With invariant features it is largely preserved (\val{main/ma_inv/heldout_long/verify_rate_cue1}).

\subsection{The residual gap: spending slack that long chains need}
\label{sec:residual}

The invariant agents still lose ground on the longest chains (\val{main/ma_inv/train_like/success} $\to$ \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_long/success} $\to$ \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success}). Some decline is intrinsic: with the same slack, longer chains have more steps to cover, and even the exact planner falls from \val{main/optimal/train_like/success} to \val{main/optimal/heldout_long/success} and \val{main/optimal/heldout_xlong/success}. The cue-triggered heuristic barely declines (\val{main/cue_triggered/train_like/success} $\to$ \val{main/cue_triggered/heldout_long/success} $\to$ \val{main/cue_triggered/heldout_xlong/success}), because it never spends slack on un-cued steps. The budget-aware heuristic, tuned on training lengths, degrades like the learned agent (\val{main/budget_aware_1/train_like/success} $\to$ \val{main/budget_aware_1/heldout_long/success} $\to$ \val{main/budget_aware_1/heldout_xlong/success}), which suggests that part of the problem is how the budget is allocated rather than the representation. The six units of slack beyond the chain length must cover every cued check plus one repair. Two ways of spending slack are candidates:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Un-cued checks.} On short chains, slack is left over for checking un-cued steps, which pays off. On long chains there are more cued steps to cover, and slack spent early is missing later. The budget-aware heuristic loses ground in exactly this way.
  \item \textbf{Non-repair \texttt{REVISE}s.} The learned agents also spend slack on \texttt{REVISE}s that do not follow an \textsc{unsafe} check (\val{main/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/nonrepair_revise_count} per episode at 10--14, each costing two units with its re-execution). This is a consequence of the memoryless observation (\S\ref{sec:setup}): after a \textsc{safe} check, undoing and redoing a step leads to the same observation as a genuine repair.
\end{itemize}
Fault detection at 10--14 is \val{main/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/fault_detected} for the learned agent and \val{main/budget_aware_1/heldout_xlong/fault_detected} for the budget-aware heuristic, against \val{main/cue_triggered/heldout_xlong/fault_detected} for the cue-triggered one. The exact planner shows that the best allocation depends on how many steps remain. At the same slack and cue, whether checking a step is optimal changes with the number of remaining steps (Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:optimal}): with most of the slack left, the planner checks un-cued steps when few steps remain but not when many do. A policy that sees only slack and cue, as the invariant encoder provides, cannot follow this rule at every length. Which of the two ways of spending slack costs the learned agents most is still a hypothesis: \S\ref{sec:followups} removes most non-repair \texttt{REVISE}s with a memory bit and finds no held-out gain, so that part of the explanation is at best partial.

\subsection{Robustness to environment shifts}
\label{sec:shifts}

Table~\ref{tab:shifts} evaluates policies at lengths 7--9 under shifts they were not trained on.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Fault rate.} The invariant monitorability-aware agent is robust to the fault rate (\val{main/ma_inv/fault_0.6/success} at $p_f{=}0.6$, \val{main/ma_inv/fault_0.9/success} at $p_f{=}0.9$).
  \item \textbf{Cue quality.} It tracks the cue-triggered heuristic when cue quality changes (clean: \val{main/ma_inv/cue_clean/success} vs.\ \val{main/cue_triggered/cue_clean/success}; noisy: \val{main/ma_inv/cue_noisy/success} vs.\ \val{main/cue_triggered/cue_noisy/success}). This confirms that the policy relies on the cue rather than on a fixed checking schedule.
  \item \textbf{Verification cost.} It degrades when checks cost more than in training (\val{main/ma_inv/verify_cost_3/success} at $c_V{=}3$, against \val{main/cue_triggered/verify_cost_3/success} for the cue-triggered heuristic and \val{main/optimal/verify_cost_3/success} for the planner), because it keeps checking at a rate the budget no longer supports.
  \item \textbf{Budget slack.} It is sensitive to the slack in \emph{both} directions. With slack 4, success falls to \val{main/ma_inv/budget_extra_4/success} (cue-triggered: \val{main/cue_triggered/budget_extra_4/success}; planner: \val{main/optimal/budget_extra_4/success}), although every slack value then stays inside the training range. This is the allocation problem of \S\ref{sec:residual} again: a checking rate tuned to slack 6 runs out of budget. With slack 8, success falls to \val{main/ma_inv/budget_extra_8/success}. Here the budget-aware heuristic improves (\val{main/budget_aware_1/budget_extra_8/success}), and the cue-triggered heuristic, which never spends extra slack, is unchanged (\val{main/cue_triggered/budget_extra_8/success}). With slack 8 the slack feature also takes values never seen in training. The ``invariant'' encoder is invariant to length, not to budget.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Training stability and sensitivity}
\label{sec:stability}
\label{sec:sensitivity}

DQN agents in this environment are unstable across checkpoints. This is related to the policy churn of \citet{schaul2022churn}, which they measure as changes in greedy actions between consecutive updates; we measure it as swings in evaluated performance over thousands of episodes. Figure~\ref{fig:churn} plots greedy success for each seed of the two monitorability-aware conditions. Policies repeatedly switch, within a few thousand episodes, between a solution that checks and repairs and one that checks but does not repair (\S\ref{sec:norepair}). The probe of \S\ref{sec:norepair} suggests why: the two solutions differ in one greedy choice whose learned values are nearly tied. After the first 5{,}000 episodes, the mean absolute change in training-length success between consecutive evaluations is \val{main/ma_inv/mean_abs_change} for the invariant monitorability-aware agent. That is larger than for outcome-only agents (\val{main/outcome_inv/mean_abs_change}), which mostly sit in the never-check regime.

Because of this churn, last-checkpoint results (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:final}) are lower and noisier than selected-checkpoint results. For example, the invariant monitorability-aware agent scores \ci{main/ma_inv@final/heldout_long/success} instead of \ci{main/ma_inv/heldout_long/success} at lengths 7--9. With last checkpoints, the results split three ways:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Unchanged:} the encoder gap ($+$\val{diff/ma_inv@final-ma_abs@final/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14, $p=\val{diff/ma_inv@final-ma_abs@final/heldout_xlong/success/p}$) and the advantage of the monitorability-aware reward over outcome-only ($+$\val{diff/ma_inv@final-outcome_inv@final/heldout_long/success} at 7--9, $p=\val{diff/ma_inv@final-outcome_inv@final/heldout_long/success/p}$).
  \item \textbf{Weaker:} the difference between naive process reward and outcome-only, which was not clearly resolved at selected checkpoints either ($p=\val{diff/naive_inv-outcome_inv/heldout_long/success/p}$ at 7--9), shrinks to $+$\val{diff/naive_inv@final-outcome_inv@final/heldout_long/success} [\val{diff/naive_inv@final-outcome_inv@final/heldout_long/success/lo}, \val{diff/naive_inv@final-outcome_inv@final/heldout_long/success/hi}].
  \item \textbf{Lost:} closeness to the cue-triggered heuristic. The last checkpoint's difference from it is \val{diff/ma_inv@final-cue_triggered/heldout_long/success} [\val{diff/ma_inv@final-cue_triggered/heldout_long/success/lo}, \val{diff/ma_inv@final-cue_triggered/heldout_long/success/hi}] at lengths 7--9, and the ranking of the reward ablations changes (\S\ref{sec:ablations}).
\end{itemize}
Selection uses only training-length validation episodes, so it cannot favour the encoder that generalizes better.
