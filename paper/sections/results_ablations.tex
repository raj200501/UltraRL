\subsection{Which reward terms matter}
\label{sec:ablations}

Table~\ref{tab:ablations} removes one group of terms at a time from the monitorability-aware reward, with invariant observations and 10 seeds each. Every variant is evaluated on the same episodes as the full reward, so the differences below are paired.

\paragraph{Learning to check needs a reward for checks, but not a particular one.}
Every variant that keeps the per-action cost and \emph{some} payment for informative checks reaches 0.88--0.89 success on training lengths. That holds with the cue terms removed, with the safe/fault check bonuses removed, with the cue bonus alone, and with the cue, gap and waste terms all removed (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}), against \val{main/outcome_inv/train_like/success} for outcome-only. None of the variants changes how the agents fail during training. No variant ever collapses to never checking (at most \val{ablations/cue_bonus_only/collapse_nevercheck_rate/pct} of late checkpoints). Every variant shows the detect-without-repair collapse of \S\ref{sec:norepair}, between \val{ablations/no_check_bonuses/collapse_rate/pct} (no check bonuses) and \val{ablations/no_budget_penalty/collapse_rate/pct} (no per-action cost) of late checkpoints. The collapse therefore does not hinge on any one term.

\paragraph{Generalization depends on not over-checking.}
On longer chains the variants separate.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Cue bonus alone.} With every other process term removed, the cue bonus is indistinguishable from the full reward (\val{ablations/cue_bonus_only/heldout_long/success} at 7--9 and \val{ablations/cue_bonus_only/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14, vs.\ \val{ablations/ma_inv/heldout_long/success} and \val{ablations/ma_inv/heldout_xlong/success}).
  \item \textbf{Removing only the cue terms.} This is by far the most damaging change: $-$\val{abl/ma_inv-no_cue_terms/heldout_long/success} [\val{abl/ma_inv-no_cue_terms/heldout_long/success/lo}, \val{abl/ma_inv-no_cue_terms/heldout_long/success/hi}] at 7--9 and $-$\val{abl/ma_inv-no_cue_terms/heldout_xlong/success} at 10--14 ($p=\val{abl/ma_inv-no_cue_terms/heldout_xlong/success/p}$). At 10--14 this variant falls below outcome-only. Removing the cue terms \emph{together with} the gap and waste terms costs much less ($-$\val{abl/ma_inv-no_monitor_terms/heldout_long/success} at 7--9).
  \item \textbf{The interaction.} The gap term refunds any check that closes the unchecked gap, cued or not. Without the cue terms to counterbalance it, agents check un-cued steps twice as often (\val{ablations/no_cue_terms/heldout_long/verify_rate_cue0} vs.\ \val{ablations/ma_inv/heldout_long/verify_rate_cue0} at 7--9). That costs little on short chains but spends the slack that long chains need (\S\ref{sec:residual}).
  \item \textbf{Other terms.} Removing the per-action cost also hurts on held-out lengths ($-$\val{abl/ma_inv-no_budget_penalty/heldout_long/success}, $p=\val{abl/ma_inv-no_budget_penalty/heldout_long/success/p}$ at 7--9), as does removing the safe/fault check bonuses ($-$\val{abl/ma_inv-no_check_bonuses/heldout_long/success}). Removing the gap term or the waste penalty alone has no resolvable effect.
  \item \textbf{No \texttt{REVISE}.} Without \texttt{REVISE}, a caught fault cannot be repaired and success falls to the never-check level (\val{ablations/no_revise/train_like/success}).
\end{itemize}
